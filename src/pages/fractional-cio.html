Fractional CIO

Technology leadership that turns priorities into action.

Technology decisions affect nearly every part of the business, from security and operations to budgeting, growth, and AI adoption. But most small businesses and nonprofits do not need, and could not justify, a full-time CIO.

Frontline provides experienced technology leadership that works alongside owners, directors, whatever internal IT you have, and your existing technology providers.

More than IT advice

Technology decisions executives can act on

Frontline translates technical issues into business conversations.

The objective is not simply to recommend more technology. It is to give leadership a clear understanding of five things:

  1. Where are we today?
  2. Where are we going?
  3. What are the risks?
  4. What needs to happen next?
  5. What should we spend, and why?

That creates a practical framework for making technology decisions based on business priorities rather than reacting to the next urgent issue.

How the work is sequenced

From current state to a plan the business can fund.

  1. Understand

    Environment, risks, vendors, costs, documentation, and the business direction behind them.

  2. Prioritize

    Rank the work by business impact, risk, and dependency, not by whatever broke last.

  3. Plan & budget

    Short- and long-term roadmaps with investment priorities leadership can approve.

  4. Lead & review

    Ongoing oversight, executive reviews, vendor management, and course correction.

Strategy backed by execution

Many advisory firms stop at recommendations.

Frontline combines strategic technology leadership with engineers experienced in cloud, cybersecurity, infrastructure, systems, and modern IT operations.

If a roadmap identifies outdated infrastructure, security gaps, undocumented systems, cloud issues, access-control problems, or other technical deficiencies, Frontline can help design and execute the remediation.

The result is a technology strategy that can move from planning to implementation without losing context between the people recommending the work and the people responsible for delivering it.
